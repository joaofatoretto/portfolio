# Who hires João, and how

Summary of the persona research behind the site. The full maps (blueprint, four journeys with steps, thoughts, feelings, pain points, opportunities) live in the Figma file **Frequency**, page **Portfolio · Journeys**.

> These are proto-personas and proto-journeys, built from career.md and common hiring practice, not from interviews yet. Time budgets are estimates.

## The four people

| Persona | The question they bring | Time with the site | Track | Wins them | Loses them |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recruiter** | Can I explain this person to my hiring manager in one sentence? | ~10 s, then 1–3 min | Both | Plain title, years, known names, numbers, CV in one click | Clever copy that hides the role, slow intro, unclear time zone |
| **Design leader** | How does this person think when nobody hands them the problem? | 1–3 min, then 10–20 min on one case | Product Designer | Problem framing, honest role, real tradeoffs, measured results | Final screens only, process theatre, "we" with no "I" |
| **Tech leader** | Does "ships code" mean production code, or a bit of HTML? | ~2 min, longer if there's code | Design Engineer | Live products, named stack, architecture, tests, honest status | A vague "I code too", AI hype, no links |
| **Founder / CEO** | Will this person move my business, and fast? | ~1 min on a phone, then a call | Both, as outcomes | Business results, speed, ownership, idea to MVP | Design jargon, no outcomes, signs they won't stay |

## The hiring blueprint
Nine phases: a role opens → discover → **first signal** → screen → **evaluate** → share → contact → **interview** → decide. The three in bold are the moments of truth. In four phases (first signal, screen, evaluate, share) João isn't in the room: **the content has to sell on its own.**

## Nine rules for the content
1. **Say the role before the hook.** "Listen." earns attention; role, years and a value line earn the click, readable in under 1 s.
2. **Proof before process.** A proof strip under the hero: real numbers and known names.
3. **Every case starts with a 20-second summary:** problem, role, team, time, result. Same block, same place.
4. **Show where the brief changed.** The strongest seniority signal (e.g. the CTO's countdown timer at Superopa).
5. **Make "ships code" checkable.** Live products, stack, architecture, tests, how AI output is reviewed.
6. **Answer logistics up front.** Remote, US and Canadian teams, fluent English, availability, a text-based CV. No location.
7. **Be honest where numbers are missing.** Say what changed and what wasn't measured; label each product's real status.
8. **Built to be forwarded.** Deep links, one page per case, a quotable one-liner.
9. **The intro never blocks the content.** The tune-in plays once, behind the words. Mobile first.
